Playbook

{{ app_name }} finds what outperformed. What you do with it is judgement — here is the judgement, written down.

1 Choosing which winner to work from

Don't just take the top number

The highest multiple is the loudest post in your feed, not automatically the right one for you. Read down the list for the one you could plausibly have written yourself. A 28× post on a subject you have no standing to talk about will land worse than a 4× post that is genuinely yours — your audience can tell the difference immediately, even when they could not explain how.

Ask whether the mechanic repeats

Some posts win on a moment — a news story, a bit of drama, a photo that happened once. Those are unrepeatable and there is nothing in them to reuse. The question to ask of any winner is: would this still work next month with different specifics? If the answer is no, keep scrolling.

Read the mix, not the total

Two posts with identical totals can have done completely different things, and they call for different follow-ups:

  • Comments dominate — it was answerable. A question, a gap, an opinion worth arguing with, an invitation to share your own version.
  • Shares dominate — it was useful, validating, or something people wanted to be seen agreeing with. A different lever entirely.
  • Reactions only — people agreed on sight and moved on. The hook did all the work and the body did not ask anything of them.

Decide which of those you actually want before you pick a post to model.

Mind the young posts and the thin groups

A post under 48 hours old is flagged as recent because its numbers are still filling in — a high multiple there may not survive the week. And a group needs at least {{ min_sample }} measured posts before its median means anything; below that, {{ app_name }} says so rather than scoring it, and you should treat those groups as unranked rather than unimpressive.

2 Making it yours

Never post it word for word

Two reasons, and the second matters more. First, you are usually posting into the same group it came from, in front of the same people who saw the original — they will recognise it. Second, it would not work anyway: a large part of why that post landed is who posted it and when. The words are the least transferable thing about it. You are reusing the mechanic, not the copy.

Flip it

The strongest and easiest reframe: keep the subject, reverse the stance. If the winner was somebody saying what they cannot stand about something, yours is why you have quietly come to love it. Same nerve, opposite hand on it — and the people who agreed with the original will argue with yours, which is the same engagement arriving through a different door.

Other turns on the same idea:

  • Agree and extend — "everyone's saying X, and here's the part nobody's mentioned."
  • The counterexample — the case where the popular advice fails.
  • Change the altitude — a broad question that worked becomes one specific story, or a specific story becomes the general pattern.
  • Change who it's for — the same insight aimed at the beginner instead of the veteran reads as a completely different post.

Steal the shape, not the subject

Often the transferable thing is the format rather than the topic: the list of things you stopped doing, the confession, the before and after, the question that hides a false binary, the number nobody expected. Those shapes work across subjects. Note the shape, then fill it with your own material.

One mechanic per post

When three different posts win in a group, the temptation is to combine them. Don't — a post doing three clever things at once usually does none of them clearly. Pick the mechanic, build the whole post on it, and keep the other two for next week.

3 Working with the AI

Your own GPT may write it better than ours — use both

This is worth saying plainly rather than pretending otherwise. If you have an assistant you have been training for months — your own GPT, your own project, your own custom instructions — it knows your voice far better than anything here can, because you taught it.

The two know different things, so use each for what it has:

  • {{ app_name }} knows the data — which post beat its group, by how much, what the engagement mix says about why, and which mechanic is worth transplanting.
  • Your assistant knows your voice — the rhythm, the vocabulary, the things you would never say.

A workflow that works well: find the winner here, screenshot it or copy the copy, drop it into your own assistant with your usual instructions, and let it write in the voice it already knows. Use {{ app_name }} to decide what to write, and whichever writer you trust most to actually write it.

Fill in your brand profile

Remix reads the profile in Settings — what you do, who you write for, how you sound. Without it, it can only anchor on the group the post came from, so it is guessing at your subject. With it, the variants come back about your world instead of somebody else's.

Use the steer field, and be blunt in it

The Steer it box on any post outranks everything else in the prompt. One line is enough — "keep it about title work, no emoji, much shorter". If a variant comes back wrong, steer it rather than regenerating and hoping.

The blanks in square brackets are deliberate

Remix will not invent your clients, your numbers or your history, so where a first-person post needs a specific it leaves you [the deal that fell through] to fill. Fill those with something true. That one concrete detail is usually the entire reason a personal post lands, and it is the one thing no model can supply for you.

4 Building the habit

Capture more than feels necessary

Scoring is comparative — a median needs a sample before it means anything. Browsing your groups normally for a week with the extension on gives you a far more trustworthy baseline than a single deliberate scan, and it costs you nothing extra because you were going to scroll anyway.

Keep a swipe file

Save the posts you would genuinely write a version of, not just the ones with big numbers. Over a month that becomes a shortlist of formats proven in your groups — which is worth more than any generic list of hooks, because it was measured where you actually post.

Watch your own posts too

Scan your own page or profile and your posts get captured and scored on the same terms as everyone else's. That closes the loop: you can see whether the version you wrote actually beat the group, instead of guessing.

None of this is a rule. It is what tends to work — if something here consistently doesn't for your audience, believe your audience.
