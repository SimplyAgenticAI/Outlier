{# ------------------------------------------------- the actual difference #}
Why the ranking is different

A big number and a breakout number are not the same thing

Sort by raw engagement and the biggest groups win every time — you end up reading a 40,000-member group's average Tuesday. {{ app_name }} measures how far each post beat the typical post in its own group, then ranks on that multiple. A 900-reaction post in a group that usually sees 800 is noise. A 240-reaction post in a group that usually sees 40 is a formula worth stealing.

Same feed, both groups. The one on the left is the post worth reading — and on a raw-engagement leaderboard it never appears.

Once you've found it

Turn a winner into your next post

Remix

Take a post that beat its group and get variants that reuse the mechanic that made it work — the same hook shape, your subject, new specifics. It reads the engagement mix too: a post that drew comments did something answerable, and a post that drew shares did something worth forwarding. Ask questions of everything you've captured. Which group is worth your time, what keeps working in it, what you posted last month that is worth doing again. It answers from your own data, not from generic advice about posting at 9am.

{# ------------------------------------------------------------ the limit #}
The honest boundary

It only ever reads what you can already see

{{ app_name }} is a browser extension. It reads the posts on the pages you visit while you visit them — the groups you joined, the pages you follow. It does not sign into anything on your behalf, and it cannot reach a group you are not a member of. That is the real limit of the tool, and it is also the reason you can run it on your own account without wondering what it is doing when you are not looking.
